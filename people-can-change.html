People Can Change

A Bible Study on Transformation
"Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, the new creation has come: The old has gone, the new is here!" 2 Corinthians 5:17
We've all heard it — "people don't change." Maybe we've said it ourselves. But Scripture tells a different story. From hearts of stone replaced with hearts of flesh, to minds renewed and old selves put away, the Bible insists that transformation is not only possible — it's the point. This study walks through five passages that describe what change looks like, where it comes from, and what it asks of us.
All Scripture quotations from the NIV. One session.

Opening

  1. When you hear someone say "people don't change," do you believe that? Why or why not?

The New Creation

Paul doesn't describe change as improvement or self-help. He calls it a new creation — the old gone, something entirely new standing in its place. That's a bold claim. It raises the question: if the old has truly gone, why do we still feel its pull?

2 Corinthians 5:17
Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, the new creation has come: The old has gone, the new is here!

This isn't a promise of perfection — it's a declaration of identity. The foundation has shifted, even when the building is still under construction.

Reflect

  1. In 2 Corinthians 5:17, Paul says the old "has gone." Is that instant or a process? What has that looked like in your own life?

The Heart Transplant

Ezekiel writes to a people in exile — displaced, broken, far from home. And God's promise isn't "try harder." It's "I will do this." He removes the stone. He puts in the flesh. He places His Spirit inside. The surgery is His.

Ezekiel 36:26–27
I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit in you; I will remove from you your heart of stone and give you a heart of flesh. And I will put my Spirit in you and move you to follow my decrees and be careful to keep my laws.

Notice the sequence: new heart, then new obedience. The change in behavior follows the change in nature — not the other way around.

Reflect

  1. Ezekiel 36:26–27 says God does the heart transplant — He removes the stone, He puts in His Spirit. What's our part if He's doing the surgery?

As Far as the East Is from the West

David writes as someone who has needed — and received — deep mercy. Psalm 103 isn't abstract theology. It's the testimony of a man who knows what he's been forgiven of, and it's overwhelming.

Psalm 103:8–12
The Lord is compassionate and gracious, slow to anger, abounding in love. He will not always accuse, nor will he harbor his anger forever; he does not treat us as our sins deserve or repay us according to our iniquities. For as high as the heavens are above the earth, so great is his love for those who fear him; as far as the east is from the west, so far has he removed our transgressions from us.

If God has removed our sins that far, the question becomes: why do we keep going back to pick them up?

Reflect

  1. Psalm 103 says God removes our sins "as far as the east is from the west." If God has done that, why do we still carry guilt and shame around?

Clothe Yourselves

Paul shifts from the theological to the practical. If you've been raised with Christ, here's what you put on — like getting dressed in the morning. It's deliberate, daily, and specific.

Colossians 3:12–15
Therefore, as God's chosen people, holy and dearly loved, clothe yourselves with compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness and patience. Bear with each other and forgive one another if any of you has a grievance against someone. Forgive as the Lord forgave you. And over all these virtues put on love, which binds them all together in perfect unity. Let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts, since as members of one body you were called to peace. And be thankful.

The metaphor of clothing is intentional — these aren't personality traits you're born with. They're things you choose to put on. Every day.

Reflect

  1. Colossians 3:12–15 gives a practical list — compassion, kindness, humility, patience, forgiveness. Which one is hardest for you right now, and why?

The Renewing of Your Mind

Romans 12 sits at the hinge of Paul's letter — after eleven chapters of theology, he turns to life. And the first instruction isn't an action. It's a transformation of how you think.

Romans 12:2
Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind. Then you will be able to test and approve what God's will is — his good, pleasing and perfect will.

The word "transformed" here is metamorphoo — the same word used for Jesus' transfiguration. It's not a minor adjustment. It's a fundamental change in form.

Reflect

  1. Romans 12:2 says transformation comes through "renewing of your mind." What does that actually look like day to day?

Closing

  1. Last night we spoke about change, almost unrecognizable change. Think back in the last 2–3 years — how have you changed? What motivated or powered that change?

This Week

Pick one item from Colossians 3:12–15 — compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, patience, forgiveness, love — and focus on putting it on deliberately each morning this week. At the end of the week, notice what changed.

"Be transformed by the renewing of your mind." Not improved. Not adjusted. Transformed. The God who replaces hearts of stone with hearts of flesh is still in the business of making people new.
