Discerning God's Best

When the future is a closed book

We all want the cheat code. The one right college major, the one right job, the one right person to spend our life with, revealed in advance so we can stop second-guessing. Underneath that wanting is a quiet fear: that there is a single correct path, that we might miss it, and that missing it would put us outside God's best for good.

This study sits with a harder yet freer idea. That God's intention for your life is real, that he may rarely tell you exactly which step to take, and that his love is big enough to find you no matter how far you stray. Three truths, one part for each.

Read together, reflect privately.

Opening

  1. Tell the group about a decision you were sure about that went sideways, or one you were unsure about that turned out well.

Part 1 · Trials and His Timing

James 1:2-4
Consider it pure joy, my brothers and sisters, whenever you face trials of many kinds, because you know that the testing of your faith produces perseverance. Let perseverance finish its work so that you may be mature and complete, not lacking anything.

James says trials can build something in us, perseverance. But hold it next to Job, who lost everything and was never told why. Then consider that James also says God does not tempt anyone with evil.

James 1:13
When tempted, no one should say, 'God is tempting me.' For God cannot be tempted by evil, nor does he tempt anyone.

So trials sometimes forge perseverance, sometimes their reason stays hidden in our lifetime, and sometimes they do not come from God at all. And God's timing is not ours. In Jeremiah 29 the prophet speaks for God, instructing the people on how to live as they go into exile:

Jeremiah 29:10-11
When seventy years are completed for Babylon, I will come to you and fulfill my good promise to bring you back to this place. For I know the plans I have for you, declares the LORD, plans to prosper you and not to harm you, plans to give you hope and a future.

Seventy years in exile. Most of those people would not live to see these plans come to life. A faithful long arc, not a quick rescue. Be encouraged, though. There are examples where God can be explicit in his direction.

1 Kings 19:4
He came to a broom bush, sat down under it and prayed that he might die. 'I have had enough, LORD,' he said. 'Take my life; I am no better than my ancestors.'
1 Kings 19:11-12
The LORD said, 'Go out and stand on the mountain in the presence of the LORD, for the LORD is about to pass by.' Then a great and powerful wind tore the mountains apart and shattered the rocks before the LORD, but the LORD was not in the wind. After the wind there was an earthquake, but the LORD was not in the earthquake. After the earthquake came a fire, but the LORD was not in the fire. And after the fire came a gentle whisper.

And in that whisper God gave Elijah specific marching orders, go and anoint Hazael, Jehu, and Elisha, and he told him he was not alone, seven thousand in Israel had not bowed to Baal. Thankfully God does not wait for us to have perfect faith before he gives insight. He has great power, yet his direction may come as a quiet whisper. So keep seeking him out.

Reflect

  1. When a season is hard and drags on, how do you tell whether it is something God is using or a sign to change direction? And could you be at peace even if you never fully knew which?
A secular echo (not Scripture)

Viktor Frankl, a Viennese psychiatrist who survived the Nazi camps, wrote that meaning can be found even in unavoidable suffering, often in the stance we choose to take toward it. His line, borrowed from Nietzsche: those who have a why to live can bear almost any how.

Part 2 · No Cheat Sheets

Psalm 139:16
All the days ordained for me were written in your book before one of them came to be.

This verse tempts us to look over God's shoulder, to know the perfect path to take. It can leave you feeling that God keeps every move you should make in a secret book, and that if you ask nicely enough he will slip you the answer key. But look a few verses up:

Psalm 139:7-10
Where can I go from your Spirit? Where can I flee from your presence? If I go up to the heavens, you are there; if I make my bed in the depths, you are there. If I rise on the wings of the dawn, if I settle on the far side of the sea, even there your hand will guide me, your right hand will hold me fast.

The author is not speaking of one perfect path. Instead, there is nowhere you can go, no turn you can take, where God is not already there, ready to guide and hold you. So yes, he knows your days, but those are for him to hold. Still, we should seek out his will, and Paul shows how to approach that.

Romans 12:2
Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind. Then you will be able to test and approve what God's will is.

Notice the order. Discernment comes after you have changed, not before. You do not crack a code. You become someone who can test and recognize God's will. Paul knew radical transformation firsthand, given his own road to Damascus.

Reflect

  1. If God is not hiding the one correct answer, the one best path, something for you to possibly get wrong, then does a big decision feel lighter to you, or heavier? What might you do differently if you believed there were several good paths in front of you, not one secret right one?
A secular echo (not Scripture)

Two philosophers circled the same ground. L.A. Paul studies transformative choices, like marriage or parenthood, and points out that you cannot really know what they will be like until you are inside them, and they change who you are once you are. So discernment in advance is genuinely hard. Ruth Chang argues that some choices are not a case of one being secretly better. They are good in different ways, with nothing to break the tie, and when reasons run out like that, you do not discover the right answer. You make it right by committing to it.

Part 3 · Grace When You Choose Wrong

Some decisions are enormous. A few are so big they become a promise, a commitment made to God. Marriage is one. A calling can be another. Discernment does not end the moment you choose; the biggest choices bind you, and part of what makes them frightening is the fear of one day failing the promise. So a study on discerning God's path has to ask the question directly: what is God's response when we break those promises, when we transgress against him?

Luke 15:18, 20, 24
I will set out and go back to my father and say to him: Father, I have sinned against heaven and against you. ... But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and was filled with compassion for him; he ran to his son, threw his arms around him and kissed him. ... For this son of mine was dead and is alive again; he was lost and is found.

This is from Jesus' parable of the lost son, told in response to criticism that he spent his time with sinners. The son had taken his inheritance early, which in that culture was as good as telling his father 'I wish you were dead,' and squandered it. When the father sees him returning, he runs anyway. A dignified man of that time did not run; the father took on the shame to reach his boy first. And he runs while the son is still far off, before a word of apology.

Hold one thing steady, though. The son comes to his senses, repents, and says, 'I have sinned' (15:21). The father celebrates the reunion, not the transgression. This is not grace that shrugs at the wreckage. It is grace that refuses to let the wreckage be the last word. By every fair reckoning, a broken promise should disqualify you. God runs to embrace you anyway.

Reflect

  1. Is there a choice you quietly worry was the wrong one in God's eyes? What changes if the worst case is not being disqualified, but a Father already running toward you?
A secular echo (not Scripture)

Annie Duke, a former professional poker player turned decision researcher, warns against 'resulting,' judging a decision only by how it turned out. A good decision can still end badly, and a bad outcome does not prove you chose wrong.

This Week

Name the decision or season in front of you, and one small step of trust you can take before you meet again. Then take it.

Three truths, and not one of them is a map. God is faithful even when his timing outruns your patience. There is no secret right path you can miss, only a God who holds all your days and slowly changes you into someone who can see. And when you break a promise along the way, he runs toward you, not away.

So take none of this as the answer key. The point is that the sheet was never handed over. Go to his word, listen for the whisper, and trust that the discerning is yours to do, with him.
